Two lines that explain a queue to a 10-year-old. Line 1 is one vivid sentence built on a single analogy — a queue as a slow caterpillar made of people. Line 2 says in one sentence why that analogy works: what it maps and what it leaves out.

> A queue is a slow caterpillar made of people, shuffling forward one tiny step at a time until its head reaches the front.
> It maps how everyone moves in one line and waits their turn, but it leaves out that real caterpillars never get bored, push ahead, or need a ticket when they arrive.

## Why this pair

- **Line 1 explains, not just describes:** a 10-year-old can picture a caterpillar, stand in a queue, and feel why it creeps forward step by step instead of rushing.
- **One analogy only:** no list of comparisons, no second metaphor crowding the picture — the caterpillar carries the whole sentence.
- **Line 2 is honest about the limits:** it names three concrete places where the analogy stops (boredom, cutting in line, paying at the front) instead of praising the comparison.
- **The pair works together:** the second line explains the first rather than repeating it, in words a 10-year-old follows without being talked down to.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```

Writes the two lines (287 characters) to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` (default `/output`). Pure Python standard library, no dependencies. The program self-checks the entry before writing: UTF-8, 1–400 characters, exactly two lines, one sentence per line, no leading/trailing whitespace.
